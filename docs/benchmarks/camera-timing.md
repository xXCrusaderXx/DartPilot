# Camera timing

How synchronous the three reference cameras are (#20, technical plan 5.1 experiment 1), measured on the game 20261025T080221Z-91cb (14 min), the detection test 20261025T093140Z-9406 (73 min), and a timing recording 20261025T215100Z-1c5d (2 min) with a phone screen flashing at the board. The tables are in [camera-timing-data.md](camera-timing-data.md), generated by:

```sh
cd tools
python -m dartpilot_tools.timing <sessions>/20261025T080221Z-91cb <sessions>/20261025T093140Z-9406 <sessions>/20261025T215100Z-1c5d --out ../docs/benchmarks/camera-timing-data.md
```

## Findings

- **The cameras run free, at the same rate.** Each exposes every 33.328 ms on its mapped PTS clock (ADR 0005), without measurable jitter. Between the cameras, the frame grids keep a constant offset: drift at most 0.005 ms per minute, jitter around offset and drift 2–34 µs. On this computer, the cameras' clocks agree far better than the 2–89 ppm found on the phone (#104).
- **The offset is a phase within one frame, new at every start.** In the game, camera .3 exposed 10.7 ms and camera .4 14.6 ms before camera .2; in the detection test 7.2 and 11.0 ms. The pairs add up (.4 − .3 = −3.9 and −3.8 ms), so the measurement is consistent. Since the order and size depend on when each camera started, a frame of one camera and the nearest frame of another can be up to half a period apart (16.7 ms), and on average about a quarter period (8 ms) apart.
- **About 1.2 % of frames are lost per camera** (274–296 in 14 min, 598–652 in 73 min), about equally on all three.
- **The timestamps match the true exposure to about 1 ms.** As a timing reference all cameras see, a phone at the board flashed its whole screen between black and white at irregular intervals of 0.25–0.6 s (the "Kamera-Blitz" page). All three cameras saw 208 of these changes. The share of changes a camera saw one frame earlier than another measures their true phase, independently of the timestamps. Against the phase the timestamps give, the error is −0.1, −1.0 and −0.9 ms for the three pairs (±0.8–1.2 ms, 1 σ). The times at which each camera's image crossed half brightness agree on average within 0.8 ms.
- **Impacts are no timing reference.** The earlier estimate from darts landing (camera .3 5 ms late) is not confirmed by the flashes. The first frame that shows motion is sometimes the dart in flight, which the cameras do not see at the same moment. The tool now uses flashes where a session has them.

## What it means

- A still moment averages 4 frames per camera after 200 ms of stillness (#192). Phase differences of up to 17 ms do not matter for still darts: the board does not change within them.
- They matter for anything that pairs frames during motion, such as the flight of a dart, a hand at the board, or a bounce-out (#199). There, frames of the cameras are up to half a frame apart. #24 pairs frames by their mapped exposure times instead of arrival order.
